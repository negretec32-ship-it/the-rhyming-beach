For educators · University project

Teacher Corner

Pedagogical rationale, evidence, and a practical 55-minute route.

🧑‍🏫

Project overview

Literary resource
A Dog on a Log, by T. Albert

Target group
Grade 2 · ages 5–9 · A1–A2

Lesson duration
55 minutes

Main learning objective
Recognize and say rhyming pairs and use “There's a ___ on a ___!” with teacher and peer support.

The Genre-Based Approach in practice

The sequence moves from supported noticing of a model text, to co-creating a new text with the teacher, and then to children's own production. The game labels make the route visible, but the learning comes from the teacher's mediation, language choices, oral rehearsal, and observation.

🔎

1. Text deconstruction and lexicogrammatical analysis

Website: Meet the Story + Rhyme Detective.

Children do: predict from pictures, notice word-family sounds, match rhymes, and sequence setting, problem, surprise, and ending.

Evidence: correct matches, oral responses, and a sequenced story skeleton.

🧩

2. Joint construction with teacher scaffolding and mediation

Website: Build a Funny Sentence.

Children do: suggest an animal and place, listen to the teacher model sounds, and co-construct “There's a ___ on a ___!”

Evidence: a collaboratively built sentence and supported oral production.

🎭

3. Independent construction through role-play / supported communicative task

Website: Create Your Own Rhyming Scene.

Children do: choose a scene, complete a sentence strip, assign roles, rehearse with gestures, and perform for peers.

Evidence: a written sentence strip and an approximately one-minute group performance.

55-minute lesson route

5 minWarm-up & pre-teachingGreeting, known animals, key words, gestures.
10 minRead-aloudTeacher reads the original book; children predict and join the refrain.
8 minText deconstructionRhyme sorting and story skeleton.
9 minJoint constructionBuild and say a new rhyming sentence together.
15 minIndependent constructionScene card, sentence strip, rehearsal, group performance.
5 minClosing & peer feedbackKind feedback and exit ticket.

The listed stages total 52 minutes. Use the remaining approximately 3 minutes for transitions.

Teacher observation checklist

Use during the lesson. Digital success is practice evidence—not proof of independent pronunciation or language use.

CriteriaNot yetWith supportIndependently
Recognizes or says rhyming pairs
Uses “There's a ___ on a ___!”
Participates in group role-play
Takes turns and contributes respectfully
Uses a clear, audible voice when appropriate

Peer feedback prompts

💬 “Did they say the rhyme?”

📣 “Was the voice loud and clear?”

💛 “One thing I liked was…”

Use warm, specific feedback. Immediate, positive correction gives learners a safe chance to notice the sound, try again, and keep participating.
